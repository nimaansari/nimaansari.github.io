Selected work

Problem
Kids' music video is a volume game. A human studio can't make daily, on-model, on-brand videos at a cost that makes sense.
My role
Sole architect and operator: product, pipeline, budget and quality bar.
Approach
One pipeline per format (songs, alphabet, numbers, bedtime stories, sleep music), with LLM agents for scripts, scene and motion prompts, SEO and thumbnails. Generated music, voice and video are assembled with Remotion and ffmpeg. Quality gates block bad output before it ships: a vision check for character consistency, an audio loudness standard, and a pre-flight check.
Turning point
A single bad day of cascading failures cost real money. I turned it into hard rules: pre-flight checks, auto-recovery from content filters, and never losing work mid-run. After that the pipeline could be left alone.
Outcome
Eight formats on a cron schedule, about 19 public releases a week, with automatic playlists and translations into seven languages.

Problem
Teams adopting agents discover their costs after the invoice arrives. One stuck loop can burn a month's budget overnight.
My role
Designer, developer and maintainer.
Approach
A live price table across providers, budget ceilings that halt work instead of just warning, and AST analysis that flags loop patterns in agent code before it runs. It also estimates what a job will cost and suggests cheaper models that would still do it.

Published as an MIT-licensed agent skill on ClawHub with 100+ installs.

Problem
People keep thousands of screenshots (receipts, tickets, chats) and can never find the one they need.
My role
Product lead and back-end/AI engineer, working with a front-end partner.
Approach
A vision model captions each screenshot and assigns it an album. Captions and keywords are written onto the image itself (EXIF on Android, Photos captions on iOS), so nothing gets processed or paid for twice. One search box works across 25 writing systems, from Persian to Japanese.
Outcome
A working iOS and Android app with a production back end and a demo video, verified by an end-to-end test suite. The library never leaves the phone.
Why it matters
Always-on assistants are limited by energy and cost, not peak speed. If the NPU is cheap per token, agents can run locally and privately all day.
What I did
Built a benchmark that measures tokens per watt across NPU, integrated GPU and CPU, and found the NPU about twice as efficient at 8B. Then wrote my own NPU engine with open IRON/mlir-aie kernels: a 421M decision model runs at 272 ms vs 347 ms on the best CPU path, with identical decisions.
Outcome
A Linux app in beta that runs chat, vision and voice models up to 14B on the NPU behind an OpenAI-compatible API.

How I work

  1. Cost is a feature.

    Every AI system gets a budget and a cost per run before it gets a launch date.

  2. Design for the bad day.

    Pipelines fail. What matters is whether they fail loudly, recover on their own, and never lose work.

  3. Measure before you believe.

    Benchmarks with their caveats written down, evals before rollouts, and verified claims only.

  4. Leaders should still ship.

    I keep building, because good AI strategy comes from knowing exactly what the models can and can't do this month.
